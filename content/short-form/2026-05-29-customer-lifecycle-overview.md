## THE PROBLEM (20s–1min)
*[Use your hands. First-person story carries this section.]*

So here's what was happening with that founder.

He's getting 200 signups a month. 14 of them convert to paid. He does the math, figures the ads need to be better, hires a copywriter, A/B tests the landing page. Still 14 customers.

Sound familiar?

Here's the thing — most founders treat growth like it's a single problem. Traffic. Traffic fixes everything. But growth isn't one problem. It's eight.

Every customer that ever gave you money went through eight stages to get there: they discovered you, they clicked through, they signed up, they experienced your product, they converted to paid, they stayed, they expanded — and some of them referred others.

That's the customer lifecycle.

And if any one of those eight stages is broken, it doesn't matter how well the others are working. You're optimizing the wrong layer.

---

## THE FRAMEWORK (1min–2min 30s)
*[Use fingers to count. Keep it grounded — not a lecture.]*

The eight stages are: Discovery, Acquisition, Activation, Conversion, Retention, Revenue Expansion, Referral, and Reactivation.

Now most founders only ever look at two of them — Discovery and Acquisition. How do we get more eyeballs, how do we get more signups.

But here's the honest truth: fixing acquisition when your lifecycle is broken is just filling a leaking bucket faster.

The framework I use is called the SaaS Lifecycle Diagnosis. And the idea is simple — before you touch a single growth lever, you run an audit. You look at all eight stages, find which one has the most revenue sitting trapped inside it, and you fix that one first.

And there's almost always a clear order to check them in.

**First — Activation.** Are new users actually experiencing your product's core value? The average SaaS activation rate is 37.5%. Most early-stage products are below 30%. That means 7 out of 10 signups ghost you before they even understand what you built.

**Second — Trial-to-Paid Conversion.** Of the users who do get activated, how many are upgrading? If you don't know your PQL threshold — the in-app behavior that predicts conversion — you don't have a conversion system. You have hope.

**Third — Retention.** If monthly churn is above 5%, stop spending on acquisition entirely. You're running a treadmill. Fix the leak before you fill it more.

**Fourth — Revenue Expansion.** This one gets ignored the most. Expanding revenue from existing customers costs 5 to 25 times less than acquiring new ones. If your pricing has no expansion path, you have no engine — just a pipeline that always needs to be refilled.

## THE STORY PAYOFF (2min 30s–3min 15s)
*[This is the turn. Slower. Let it land.]*

Back to that founder.

We ran the lifecycle diagnosis. Turns out his trial-to-paid rate for users who actually got activated was 32%. Not bad at all.

The problem was activation. Only 44 of his 200 monthly signups were ever hitting the moment where his product clicked. The rest were disappearing at step four of his onboarding — a required API integration most users didn't have credentials ready for.

One fix. A behaviorally-triggered email that fired when a user stalled at that step — with a direct Loom walkthrough attached.

Forty-five days later, activation moved from 22% to 38%. Same $8,000 ad spend. Same traffic. 14 paid customers became 27.

He didn't need better ads. He needed a diagnosis.
