## THE PROBLEM (20s–1min)
*[Energy up. Make it feel urgent.]*

Here's what happens at most SaaS companies.

A trial starts. A clock runs. Nobody knows what the user is doing inside the product. The trial expires. An automated email goes out. No response. The founder wonders why it keeps happening.

That's not a sales problem. That's a systems problem.

You're trying to convert people you know nothing about, at the worst possible moment — after they've already moved on — with a generic "your trial is expiring" email.

The companies that convert at 35 to 45 percent? They're not sending better emails. They're sending the right trigger at the right moment to the right person.

---

## THE FRAMEWORK (1min–2min 30s)
*[Structured. Step by step.]*

This is built around one concept: the PQL — Product Qualified Lead.

A PQL is a trial user who has completed a specific in-app action sequence that correlates with paid conversion. Not someone who logged in twice. Not someone who looked at your pricing page. A user who did the specific thing that your paying customers did before they upgraded.

Here's how you find it.

**Step 1 — Run a cohort analysis.**
Take your last 50 paid conversions. Go into PostHog. Find the common in-app behavior in the 48 hours before each one upgraded. What did they all do? That pattern — that action sequence — is your PQL threshold.

**Step 2 — Build the trigger.**
Once you know the signal, you build the infrastructure around it. An automated Slack alert when a trial user hits that threshold. A two-email sequence that fires at that exact moment — not on a time delay, on a behavior trigger. For high-ACV prospects, a sales-assist task created automatically in HubSpot via n8n.

**Step 3 — Watch the rate move.**
A PQL without an automated response is just a data point. The leverage is in the trigger — the action that fires when the prospect is most ready to convert.

## REAL EXAMPLE (2min 30s–3min 30s)
*[Slow down. Specific numbers.]*

One SaaS tool I audited had an 11% trial-to-paid rate.

We ran the cohort analysis. Defined their PQL — it was users who completed three specific actions in their first 72 hours. We built a two-email sequence that fired the moment a trial hit that threshold.

60 days later: 11% became 23%. No product changes. No pricing changes. Just infrastructure.

The key insight: a conversion system isn't a better email. It's knowing who is ready to convert, the moment they're ready, and having something automated that acts on that signal.

## KEY TAKEAWAY (3min 30s–3min 50s)
*[Direct. One line.]*

Stop optimizing your trial emails. Start identifying your PQL signal. Build the trigger around it. That's the difference between 11% and 23%.
