## THE PROBLEM (20s–1min)
*[Set up the absurdity of flat pricing.]*

Here's the pricing model most early-stage SaaS companies use.

You pick a number — usually based on what felt right or what a competitor charges. You put it on a pricing page. You leave it there for 18 months. Maybe two years. You wonder why revenue growth feels like pushing a boulder.

The problem isn't the number. It's the structure.

Flat pricing has no expansion path. Every dollar of new revenue requires a new customer. Your existing customers — the ones who already trust you, already use you, already get value from you — can never pay you more even if they want to.

That's not a pricing model. That's a ceiling.

---

## THE FRAMEWORK (1min–2min 30s)
*[Clear and sequential. This should feel like a system reveal.]*

Here's the shift that changes everything: find your value metric.

A value metric is the unit of value your customers care most about — the thing that scales as they get more value from your product. Seats. API calls. Contacts. Projects. Revenue processed.

When you align your pricing tiers to your value metric, something happens: as your customers grow, they naturally move up your pricing tiers. Revenue expands without you acquiring a single new customer.

That's called net revenue retention. Companies using hybrid pricing — a fixed base plus a usage component tied to the value metric — see approximately 105% net revenue retention. That means existing customers alone are growing revenue. New customers are pure upside.

**Here's how to find your value metric in 3 steps.**

**Step 1 — Look at your best customers.**
What are your highest-value customers doing more of than your average customers? That activity — that unit — is likely your value metric.

**Step 2 — Check correlation.**
Does more of that unit correlate with higher willingness to pay? If customers who process more orders, have more seats, or make more API calls consistently stay longer and pay more — that's your metric.

**Step 3 — Add one expansion trigger.**
You don't need to redesign your entire pricing page. Start with one thing: an automated email that fires in PostHog or n8n when a customer hits 80% of their current plan's threshold. Not a generic upsell — a specific message tied to their usage. "You're approaching your limit — here's what the next tier gets you."

That one trigger adds expansion revenue without a single sales call.

## THE MATH (2min 30s–3min 15s)
*[Slow. Let the numbers breathe.]*

Think about what 105% net revenue retention actually means.

If you have $50,000 MRR today — with 105% NRR, in 12 months your existing customers alone generate $52,500. Every new customer you acquire on top of that is compounding growth, not replacement growth.

Compare that to a flat-pricing model where churn and expansion cancel each other out. Same effort. Completely different trajectory.

Expansion revenue from existing customers costs 5 to 25 times less to generate than new customer acquisition.

If your pricing has no expansion path — you're building a business that requires an endless stream of new customers just to stand still.

That's the difference between a pricing page and a pricing system.
