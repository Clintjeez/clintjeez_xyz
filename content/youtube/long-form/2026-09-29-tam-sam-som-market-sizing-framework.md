## CORE CONTENT (1:45–8:00)

### Step 1 / The Market Sizing Filter Stack (1:45–2:20)

[SLIDE 3: The Market Sizing Filter Stack — TAM → SAM → SOM as three nested filters]

"Think of your total market like an unindexed database. Every company that could theoretically use your product is a row in that table.

TAM is the whole table. SAM is a WHERE clause. SOM is a LIMIT on what your team can actually process. Most founders report the row count and call it a pitch. We're going to run the query instead.

Key insight: SOM is the only number that should ever touch your enrichment scripts or your ad targeting. Everything upstream of that is a slide, not a strategy."

### Step 2 / TAM: The Unfiltered Table (2:20–3:05)

[SLIDE 4: TAM definition — "Total Addressable Market: every possible buyer, zero competition, zero constraints"]

"TAM is the total global revenue opportunity if every potential user bought your product with zero competition. Build a project management tool, your TAM is technically every business on Earth with more than two employees.

Is that useful? For a board slide, sure — it shows investors the ceiling exists. For execution, it's noise. You cannot target 'every business on planet Earth.'

Key insight: if TAM is the only market number in your deck, that's not ambition. That's a signal you haven't segmented your market yet."

### Step 3 / SAM: The First WHERE Clause (3:05–4:05)

[SLIDE 5: SAM definition — filtered by tech stack, geography, compliance, language]

"SAM is your first real filter. It's the slice of TAM your current tech stack, geography, and compliance posture can actually service. Only integrate with Shopify, only handle US sales tax? Your SAM is Shopify merchants in North America — not 'every ecommerce business.'

This is where your ICP should come from — not from TAM. Filter by what you support today, not what your roadmap promises in Q3. Most founders find SAM is ninety percent smaller than their TAM slide implies.

Key insight: SAM is what you can serve right now, not what you're planning to serve."

### Step 4 / SOM: The LIMIT Clause (4:05–5:15)

[SLIDE 6: SOM definition — filtered by team capacity, 12–18 month realistic capture]

"SOM is the enriched, targeted subset of your SAM you can realistically capture in the next twelve to eighteen months — given your actual headcount, sales capacity, and growth budget.

This is the only number I feed into Clay, into Apollo, into paid targeting. If your SOM and your SAM are the same size, you haven't filtered by capacity — you've just relabeled your SAM. Real SOM accounts for sales cycle length, cold email deliverability, and how many qualified conversations your team can run per month.

Key insight: SOM determines your CAC and whether your growth budget is even sized correctly for the market you can reach this year."

### Step 5 / The Retention Flatline (5:15–7:15)

[SLIDE 7: The Retention Flatline — cohort curve chart, PMF line flattening vs. curve decaying to zero]

"That founder asked me the obvious next question: 'Okay, if I shrink my focus to a small SOM, how do I know when I've actually won?'

You don't guess. You look at your cohort retention curve.

Product-Market Fit is the point where your retention curve flattens and runs roughly parallel to the x-axis over a thirty, sixty, or ninety day window. Not signups. Not a launch spike on Product Hunt. The actual usage telemetry.

Pull a cohort in PostHog. Watch what happens after day thirty. If the line keeps sloping toward zero past day sixty, users are trying your product and leaving. If it flattens — even at thirty or forty percent — the users who stick are sticking for good. That's the signal a TAM slide can never show you.

Key insight: PMF isn't a launch announcement. It's the day your retention curve stops sloping down and goes flat."

### Step 6 / The Real Numbers (7:15–8:00)

[SLIDE 8: Real-world diagnostic — TAM $50B → SAM $340M → SOM $9M, retention flattening at 45% on day 45]

"Here's what that actually looked like for the founder from the hook. His deck led with fifty billion in TAM. Running it through the filter stack: his real SAM — companies on his stack, in his geographies, at his price point — was three hundred forty million. His SOM, what his two-person GTM team could actually enrich and close in a year, was nine million.

And his retention curve was still sloping down at day sixty — no flatline anywhere.

So we re-cut his cohorts by use case instead of company size, and found one segment where the curve flattened at forty-five percent by day forty-five. He raised on a SOM-anchored slide instead of a TAM-anchored one, and put the entire round's GTM budget behind the one segment that actually had a retention signal underneath it."
