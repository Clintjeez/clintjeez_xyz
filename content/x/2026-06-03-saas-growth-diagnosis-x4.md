## Post 3 — Thread
*Type: Thread | 7 tweets | Hook tweet first*

**Tweet 1 (hook):**
Stop trying to fix growth before you've run the diagnosis.

Here's the exact order to audit your SaaS lifecycle — and why the sequence matters: 🧵

**Tweet 2:**
Most SaaS founders with a growth problem try the same 3 moves: more ads, better copy, new SDR.

It doesn't work because they're fixing the wrong stage.

Growth is a system. You have to diagnose before you prescribe.

**Tweet 3:**
The SaaS Lifecycle has 8 stages:

Discovery → Acquisition → Activation → Conversion → Retention → Expansion → Referral → Reactivation

A failure in stage 3 (Activation) makes every improvement to stage 2 (Acquisition) completely irrelevant.

**Tweet 4:**
The diagnosis order:

1. Activation first — users not hitting the aha moment = everything downstream breaks
2. Conversion second — activated users not upgrading = broken trigger infrastructure
3. Retention third — churn above 5% = stop acquisition spend entirely
4. Expansion last

Fix in order. Not in whichever order feels most urgent.

**Tweet 5:**
Real case: founder spending $8K/month on LinkedIn ads.

200 signups. 14 paid conversions. 7% conversion rate.

Their instinct: ads are broken. More budget.

The diagnosis: activation rate was 22%. Only 44 users were experiencing the product. 14/44 = 32% conversion rate for activated users.

Ads were fine. Activation was the leak.

**Tweet 6:**
The fix: PostHog funnel to find the drop-off point + behaviorally-triggered Loom walkthrough email at the stall point.

45 days later: activation 22% → 38%.

Same ad spend: 14 paid conversions → 27.

Diagnosis first. Infrastructure second. Results followed.

They just didn't have the framework to read it.
