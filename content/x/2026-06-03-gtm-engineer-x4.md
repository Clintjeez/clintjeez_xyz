Most people have never heard of a GTM Engineer.

**Tweet 2:**
A GTM Engineer sits at the intersection of 3 disciplines:

→ Engineering (build systems, connect APIs, ship infrastructure)
→ Growth (acquisition, activation, conversion, retention)
→ Revenue Ops (CRM, pipeline, attribution, automation)

Not a strategist. A builder.

**Tweet 3:**
On the acquisition side, they ship:

→ Cold email infrastructure (domain setup, warmup, Clay enrichment, Instantly sequences, deliverability monitoring)
→ Programmatic SEO at scale with structured data for AI citation

The actual working system. Not a playbook.

**Tweet 4:**
On activation + conversion:

→ PostHog event taxonomy mapping the path to the aha moment
→ PQL scoring — the in-app behavior that predicts conversion
→ n8n automations that fire on behavior, not time delays

When a trial hits the PQL threshold, the sales motion triggers automatically.

**Tweet 5:**
On retention + expansion:

→ Customer health scoring in PostHog (login freq + feature adoption + support tickets)
→ Churn-risk alerts in Slack before the user cancels
→ Stripe usage triggers that prompt upgrade at the right moment

Catching churn 30 days before the cancellation event.

**Tweet 6:**
The full stack: Clay → Apollo → Instantly → HubSpot → PostHog → n8n → Stripe

Each tool is a component. The GTM Engineer makes them work as a connected system.

Any one tool used in isolation produces mediocre results. The leverage is in the orchestration.

**Tweet 7:**
