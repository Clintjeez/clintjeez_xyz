Most early-stage founders don't have a channel problem. They have an unarchitected growth motion problem — and no amount of ad spend or outbound headcount fixes a motion that was never designed.

I was on a panel a few months back when an audience member stood up. "We just launched our B2B SaaS platform," he said. "We have $50,000 left in the bank. Should we spend it on Google Ads or hire two outbound SDRs?"

Founders copy playbooks from companies with a different ACV, a different product, and a different buying process, then wonder why the CAC never works. A growth motion isn't a channel you pick. It's a system you design against your price point.

## The Growth Motion Decision Matrix

**The Growth Motion Decision Matrix is the framework that selects your growth engine — PLG, hybrid, or SLG plus ABM — based on Average Contract Value, then aligns your content stack to whichever engine you land on.** Every modern SaaS company runs on one of these engines or a hybrid blend. The mistake isn't picking the "wrong" one in the abstract — every engine works for someone. The mistake is picking one that doesn't match your ACV.

Three components define the Matrix:

1. **The engine** — PLG, hybrid Product-Assisted Growth, or SLG with an ABM overlay.
2. **The ACV band** — the contract value that makes each engine economically viable.
3. **The content stack** — TOF and BOF content built to match how that engine's buyer actually decides.

## Component 1: PLG vs. SLG — The Two Core Engines

**Product-Led Growth (PLG) is a growth engine where the product itself drives acquisition, activation, and expansion — users sign up, hit an "aha" moment, and upgrade self-serve with a credit card.** Slack, Figma, and PostHog run this motion. It depends on product telemetry, viral invite loops, and automated lifecycle email — and its advantage is brutally low CAC combined with expansion revenue that scales without headcount. Getting there requires product UX good enough to sell itself and volume high enough to make self-serve math work.

**Sales-Led Growth (SLG) is a growth engine where human reps drive acquisition through outbound, custom demos, security review, and negotiated contracts.** Snowflake and enterprise Salesforce implementations run this motion. SLG exists because complex software with high implementation costs and strict compliance requirements can't be sold through a self-serve checkout — someone has to walk the buyer through procurement. The tradeoff is real: higher CAC, longer cycles, and sales team overhead that PLG never carries.

**The key insight:** PLG and SLG aren't competing philosophies — they're solutions to different buying problems. A $200/year tool and a $150K/year platform don't share a buying process, and forcing one engine onto the wrong price point is how founders burn the runway that panel questioner was worried about.

---

## Component 2: ABM — The Sniper Overlay for Enterprise

**Account-Based Marketing (ABM) is a targeted go-to-market motion where sales and marketing build personalized experiences for a pre-vetted list of named accounts, instead of casting a wide net across an anonymous audience.** Inbound marketing catches whoever swims by. ABM decides in advance which 100 accounts matter and builds the experience around them specifically.

The mechanics are engineering, not creative: deploy reverse-IP lookup tools like Clearbit Reveal or RB2B on your site. When a visitor from a target account lands on your homepage, the headlines, logos, and case studies shift in real time to match their industry vertical. ABM isn't a campaign type — it's a personalization layer sitting on top of your SLG motion, and it only works once your ICP is defined tightly enough to name the accounts you're targeting, the same discipline behind [building an automated ICP filter for lead qualification](https://clintjeez.com/writings/lead-qualification-gateway-icp-mql-sql).

**The key insight:** ABM without a tight ICP is just expensive personalization pointed at the wrong accounts. Define who belongs on the list before you build the reverse-IP infrastructure to greet them.

## Component 3: TOF and BOF — Content That Matches the Buyer's Stage

Every growth motion needs a content stack aligned to where the buyer actually is, not a generic blog calendar. **Top of Funnel (TOF) content is the awareness layer — educational guides and frameworks built to capture search traffic and establish domain authority, not to hard-sell.** **Bottom of Funnel (BOF) content is the decision layer — product comparisons, live sandboxes, ROI calculators, and security documentation built for someone actively comparing vendors.**

The content stack should mirror your engine, not run independently of it. A PLG motion needs TOF content that funnels into a frictionless signup and BOF content that's really just a clean self-serve pricing page — no sandbox demo required when the product itself is the demo. An SLG motion needs BOF content dense enough to survive procurement review, because that's the actual decision-maker reading it.

**The key insight:** TOF content earns the visit. BOF content closes the deal. Publishing ten TOF articles a month while your BOF page is a generic "Contact Sales" button is optimizing the half of the funnel that doesn't decide revenue.

## Real-World Diagnostic

**Before:** The panel questioner had a live product, $50K left in the bank, and two options on the table — burn it on Google Ads or hire two outbound SDRs. No ACV had been defined. No engine had been chosen. Both options were built on the assumption that more spend, in either direction, would fix a motion that didn't exist yet.

**The diagnosis:** Running his situation through the Matrix exposed the real gap immediately. His product priced at $1,800/year — squarely PLG territory — but he was about to fund an SLG motion (outbound SDRs) that only makes economic sense above $50K ACV. Google Ads without a defined ICP or a self-serve activation flow would have bought clicks with nowhere qualified to land.

**The fix:** I told him to redirect the $50K entirely: zero spend on outbound headcount, zero spend on paid ads until activation was proven. Instead, invest in self-serve onboarding, instrument the product for the real "aha" moment, and put the budget behind TOF content that could compound in organic search — the same activation-first instinct behind [diagnosing a SaaS activation problem in 48 hours](https://clintjeez.com/writings/find-saas-activation-problem-48-hours) before spending on acquisition.

**The result:** He left the panel with a plan instead of a coin flip: a PLG motion matched to his actual ACV, funded by the same $50K he almost spent gambling on the wrong engine entirely.

## Implementation: How to Actually Do This

Define your ACV before you define your channel strategy. Everything downstream — engine choice, content stack, headcount decisions — is a function of that one number, and skipping it is what makes founders copy playbooks that don't fit their business.

Two mistakes show up constantly:

- **Hiring outbound SDRs for a sub-$5K ACV product.** The math never works — SDR-generated pipeline needs enterprise-scale deals to justify the fully-loaded cost.
- **Running pure PLG self-serve for a $100K+ enterprise product.** Buyers at that price point need a human to navigate security review and procurement. No amount of onboarding polish replaces that.

The meta-lesson: a growth motion is infrastructure, not inspiration. You don't need the trendiest GTM playbook. You need the one that matches the deal size your product actually supports.

---

## Where to Start

1. Calculate your real ACV — not your aspirational pricing tier, the actual average contract value you're closing today.
2. Map that number against the Decision Matrix: under $2K is PLG, $10K–$50K is hybrid Product-Assisted Growth, $100K+ is SLG with an ABM overlay.
3. If you're PLG, kill any outbound headcount spend and redirect it into self-serve onboarding and product telemetry.
4. If you're SLG, define your ICP tightly enough to name actual target accounts before you invest in ABM personalization tooling.
5. Audit your content stack against your engine: does your TOF content funnel into the right next step, and does your BOF content match what your buyer actually needs to decide?
6. Revisit the Matrix every time your ACV shifts meaningfully — a pricing change is a growth motion change, not just a billing update.

The engine isn't the exciting part of GTM. It's the part that determines whether the exciting parts — ads, content, outbound — have anywhere useful to land.

## FAQ

**How do I know if my SaaS product should be PLG or SLG?**
Check your Average Contract Value. Products under roughly $2,000/year support a PLG motion built on self-serve signup and product-led activation. Products above $100,000/year need SLG, because the buying process involves security review and procurement that no self-serve flow can replace.

**What's the difference between inbound marketing and Account-Based Marketing?**
Inbound marketing casts a wide net and qualifies whoever converts. ABM identifies a specific, pre-vetted list of target accounts first, then builds personalized experiences — like reverse-IP-triggered website content — for that named list instead of an anonymous audience.

**Can a SaaS company run PLG and SLG at the same time?**
Yes — this is the hybrid Product-Assisted Growth motion, typically used at $10K–$50K ACV. A free trial or freemium tier collects signups, and behavioral telemetry flags high-intent accounts for a sales rep to close, similar to the [MQL threshold logic in a lead qualification pipeline](https://clintjeez.com/writings/lead-qualification-gateway-icp-mql-sql).
