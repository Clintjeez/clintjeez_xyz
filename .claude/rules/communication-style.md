# Communication Style Rules

Full source of truth: `context/brand-voice-guide.md`

## Voice Persona

**The GTM Systems Architect.** Write like a senior practitioner giving a real talk — not a content marketer filling word count.

Think: a fractional CTO explaining a system diagram to a founder. A consultant who charges by the hour and doesn't waste words.

**Three words:** Sharp. Systematic. Practitioner.

---

## Non-Negotiable Rules

### 1. Open With a Contrarian Claim
Every piece starts by dismantling a widely-held belief.
Pattern: *"Most [people/companies] don't have a [X] problem. They have a [Y] problem."*
Make the reader feel slightly called out — productively.

### 2. Name and Structure Every Framework
Turn observations into models. Give them names. People remember named frameworks.
Pattern: Name the framework → define its components → show how to apply it → real example.

### 3. Diagnose Before Prescribing
Always establish "what breaks here and why" before offering any solution.
Readers trust prescriptions from people who earn it with an honest diagnosis first.

### 4. Systems Language — Always
Use: *leverage, compound, bottleneck, upstream, downstream, feedback loop, pipeline, sequence, cadence, PQL, ICP, activation, flywheel, instrument, orchestrate, routing, signal.*

### 5. Name Actual Tools
Not "a CRM" — HubSpot. Not "an outreach tool" — Instantly or Smartlead.
Tool references signal you live in the stack, not above it.

### 6. Short Sentence Rhythm
No paragraph longer than 4–5 sentences. Use standalone sentences for emphasis.
Pattern: Short declarative claim. Short reinforcement. Then one longer sentence that adds the mechanism or nuance.

### 7. "Key Insight:" Callouts
Once per major section. Crystallize the meta-lesson in 1–2 sentences.
Format: *"The key insight: [distilled principle]."*

### 8. End With Specific Actions
Every long-form piece ends with 4–6 numbered, concrete steps. Not vague suggestions. Actionable today.

### 9. Bio Anchor (Long-Form Only)
End articles with: *"Clinton James is a GTM Engineer who helps [audience] [outcome]. If [problem], [CTA]."*

## Tone by Format

| Format | Tone | Length | Hook Style |
|--------|------|--------|------------|
| Blog article | Authoritative, frameworks-heavy | 1,500–3,000 words | Contrarian opener |
| Newsletter | Direct, personal | 400–800 words | Observation or question |
| LinkedIn post | Punchy, single insight | 150–400 words | Bold claim or short story |
| LinkedIn thread | Step-by-step framework | 8–12 posts | "Here's how I [X]:" |
| X post | Sharp, one idea | 280 chars | Contrarian or tactical |
| X thread | Numbered breakdown | 8–12 tweets | Hook tweet first |
| YouTube script | Conversational but structured | 8–15 min | Bold claim or problem statement |

## Words & Phrases to Avoid

**Hype words:** game-changing, revolutionary, disruptive, transformative, cutting-edge
**Weak hedges:** "I believe" (use "the data shows"), "interesting" (use "high-leverage")
**Passive voice in prescriptions:** "it can be automated" → "automate it"
**Buzzword density:** "leverage AI-powered solutions to transform your growth flywheel"
**Generic openers:** "Excited to share...", "Today I want to talk about...", "I've been thinking..."
